# RBI-Dashboard
Excited to share my Power BI dashboard on RBI’s Average Daily Turnover, built using multi-year data covering Call Money, Government Securities, Market Repo, Forex Reserves, and Treasury Bills.
 This project helped me analyze the liquidity movements and short-term borrowing trends within the Indian financial ecosystem.
Key Insights from the Dashboard
1️⃣ Call Money Market Shows Strong Growth
From 2011 to 2025, Call Money turnover increases significantly, especially after 2017.
2025 marks the highest Call Money volume, indicating stronger interbank liquidity demand.
2️⃣ Government Securities Keep Rising Over Time
A clearly increasing trend in Total Govt Securities, showing:
Higher government borrowing
Stronger market participation
Increased investor confidence
The cumulative securities curve rises steeply post-2019.
3️⃣ Average Daily Turnover Highlights
Average Govt Securities: ₹22.15M
Average Daily Market Repo: ₹77.69M
These high numbers indicate active liquidity management by the RBI and commercial banks.
4️⃣ Total Forex Reserves
The dashboard highlights ₹17.45M, showing stable reserve management across years.
5️⃣ Yearly Call Money Comparison
Call money sees consistent upward movement, with notable spikes in:
2017
2020
2025
Sharp yearly fluctuations reflect RBI liquidity adjustments and market conditions.
6️⃣ Quarterly Behaviour of Call Money
Q3 has the highest share (33%+) of total call money—indicating seasonal liquidity pressure.
Q1 & Q4 show moderate levels, while Q2 remains relatively lower.
7️⃣ Notice Money & Treasury Bills Insights
Notice Money peaks in Q3, signalling greater mid-year borrowing needs.
Treasury Bills (91-day vs 182-day) show positive correlation, with 2017–2018 showing the strongest activity.
8️⃣ Cumulative Quarter-wise Trend
The positive contribution from all quarters leads to a steady rise in cumulative liquidity, with Q3 being the strongest driver.
Overall Conclusion
This dashboard provides a clear picture of how RBI manages short-term liquidity, government securities, and money market instruments over time.
